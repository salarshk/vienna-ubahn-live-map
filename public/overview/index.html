to “what is happening across the network?”

HOW THE SIGNALS ARE MADE

Evidence in.
Estimates out.

The platform keeps published facts, derived estimates and model outputs distinct. That separation is central to understanding what a marker or prediction can—and cannot—tell you.

01

Collect

Read public Wiener Linien departure and service-message feeds, plus published ÖBB timetable data and other connected open sources.

02

Align

Match departures to stations, lines and planned times. Keep source timestamps and known coverage gaps alongside the observations.

03

Estimate

Interpolate train movement from departure predictions and route timings. A marker describes an estimate, not a device installed on the train.

04

Evaluate

Train on earlier observations, test on later held-out data, compare with a simple baseline, and show the scores in the app.

GOOD QUESTIONS

Before you ride.

A few important details for using the map and reading its predictions.

Are the train dots exact GPS locations?

No. Wiener Linien’s public real-time feed provides departure predictions, not train GPS. U-Bahn locations are estimated from those predictions and route timing. ÖBB S-Bahn locations are scheduled timetable interpolations.

How quickly does the map refresh?

The app polls its shared snapshot frequently (about every 1.5 seconds). That describes the app’s request cycle, not how often an operator changes its source data; network delays and source updates can affect the information shown.

Can I install Vienna Rail on my phone?

Yes. The site is a Progressive Web App. On iPhone, open it in Safari, choose Share, then Add to Home Screen. GPS works only after you choose a location feature and grant permission.

Are the predictions all fully trained machine-learning models?

No. Some are rule-based or monitored baselines; other candidates may still be collecting labels or awaiting validation. Check the in-app model dashboard for current status, sample counts and scores.
